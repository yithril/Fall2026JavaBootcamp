As you are coding, ever so often, "commit" your work. What this means is, you are saving a snapshot of your code as you go along.

The cool thing about commits is that they can be reversed. Don't like your code? Hit the rewind button.

AS YOU ARE PROGRAMMING:

When you finish a thought, do the following commands. Be sure your terminal is in the same folder as your GitHub repo!

git add -A
git commit -m "What does the code you just wrote do?"

The biggest beginner mistake is to forget to put the -m after the word commit and to write a commit message. The message is important! It tells other devs what your commit is all about.

If you forget to type the -m, you might end up in the editor. Don't panic, you can still finish your commit, it'll just take longer.

-Press i to start typing.
-Type your commit message at the top.
-Press Esc.
-Type :wq
-Press Enter.

You can add and commit as many times as you want. Think of committing like finishing a thought. 

Ready to turn it in? Make sure you thoroughly test your program, ensuring that it works as expected. When you are ready to "turn it in" as it were:

git push

The push command copies your code and sends it to GitHub.com where your instructor will be able to see your code. 

You do NOT have to push every time you commit. You can commit many times and then push once. That is totally normal.
